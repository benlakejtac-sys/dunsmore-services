Part 1 — Buy the name (needs your card)

  1. Open your internet browser (Safari or Chrome) and go to: porkbun.com
    Porkbun is just a store that sells website names. Millions of businesses use it. Yes, it's really called that.
  2. In the big search box in the middle of the page, type dunsmoreservices.com and hit search.
  3. In the list that comes up, find the line that says exactly dunsmoreservices.com — around $11. Tap the + or Add to cart button on that line only.
    It will show a bunch of other endings like .net, .org, .xyz — skip all of those. You only want the .com.
  4. Tap the cart at the top of the page, then Checkout / Continue.
  5. It asks you to make an account. Use your business email: dunsmoreservicesllc@gmail.com — make up a password and write it down somewhere you won't lose it. Fill in your real name and address if it asks (that's required for owning a website name — it stays private).
  6. On the payment page, check the total: it should be about $11 for 1 year. If it offers extras — hosting, email, SSL, anything — say no to all of them. You don't need any of it. (The free "WHOIS Privacy" is fine — leave it on.)
  7. Enter your card and finish the purchase.
  8. Open your dunsmoreservicesllc@gmail.com email. If there's a message from Porkbun asking you to verify or confirm your email or contact info, open it and tap the confirm button.
    Don't skip this one — website names can get frozen if the email never gets confirmed.

Part 2 — One more account (free, no card)

  1. Go to: github.com and tap Sign up.
    GitHub is where the website's files live — think of it as the filing cabinet your website sits in. This puts that filing cabinet in your name, not Ben's.
  2. Email: dunsmoreservicesllc@gmail.com — make up a password and write this one down too.
  3. When it asks for a username, type: dunsmoreservices (Ben checked — it's available).
  4. It will email a code to your Gmail — go get the code and type it in.
  5. If it asks questions about plans or what you do, pick Free and tap Skip on anything else.

Part 3 — Hand it to Ben

  1. Text Ben: "Done with both."
  2. In a day or so you'll get one email from GitHub asking you to accept the website transfer — Ben will be on the phone with you, it's one green button. That's the moment the website officially becomes yours.
